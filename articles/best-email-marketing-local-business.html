Best Email Marketing Software for Local Businesses

A local business does not need most of what email marketing platforms advertise. A salon, a repair shop, a café, or a small clinic needs something narrower: a reliable way to collect customers' email addresses, send a monthly update or offer, ask for reviews, and remind people to come back. The four tools in this roundup — Kit, Systeme.io, Mailchimp, and Brevo — can all do that job, but they charge for it in four different ways, and their free plans have very different shapes. Choosing well is mostly about matching the billing model to the size of your list and how often you send.

The short version: Start free. Kit has the most generous free list-building plan (up to 10,000 subscribers). Brevo is the budget pick if your list is large but you send rarely — its free plan stores up to 100,000 contacts and sends 300 emails a day. Systeme.io fits if you also need booking or sales pages and funnels in the same account. Mailchimp is the familiar name with the deepest integration ecosystem, but its free plan is now the smallest of the four (250 contacts), so most businesses will outgrow it quickly.

What a local business actually needs

Before comparing logos, it helps to write down the jobs email has to do in a local business. For most shops and service businesses, the list is short:

  • A signup form for the counter, the website, or a QR code — so collecting addresses takes no staff time.
  • A regular broadcast — a monthly newsletter, seasonal offers, holiday hours, new services.
  • One or two automations — a welcome email for new subscribers, and ideally a "we miss you" or review-request email that sends itself.
  • Room to grow — a plan whose price does not punish you for the list getting bigger.
  • Simple reports — who opened, who clicked, so you know the emails are worth sending.

Everything beyond that — multivariate testing, predictive segmentation, SMS add-ons, e-commerce revenue attribution — matters to online sellers and larger teams. If a platform's pricing page leads with those features, you are probably looking at more tool than you need, priced accordingly.

Kit — the list-builder's free plan

Kit (formerly ConvertKit) is built around growing and emailing an audience. For a local business, its standout fact is the free plan: up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited landing pages, forms, and broadcasts, and no limit on how many emails you send. That is a long runway — most local lists take years to reach five figures. The catch, per Kit's help center, is that visual automations and email sequences are paid-plan features, so the free plan covers manual broadcasts but not a set-and-forget welcome series. Paid plans start at $39/month (Creator, up to 1,000 subscribers) and are priced by subscriber count, so the bill climbs as your list grows. Kit also lets you sell digital products and paid subscriptions from the same account at a flat 3.5% + 30¢ per transaction, which suits a business that wants to sell a gift card package or a downloadable guide alongside its newsletter.

Systeme.io — email inside an all-in-one

Systeme.io treats email as one part of a suite that also includes sales funnels, websites and blogs, online courses, and an affiliate program you can run yourself. Its free plan holds 2,000 contacts with unlimited email sends, 3 working sales funnels, and 1 automation rule — and it never expires and needs no credit card. Paid plans start at $17/month (Startup, 5,000 contacts). For a local business, the appeal is consolidation: the same account that sends your newsletter can host the page where customers book a package or buy a course, with 0% platform transaction fees (your payment gateway still takes its cut). The trade-off is depth: Systeme.io does not publish the email-specific analytics — engagement scoring, deliverability reporting — that email specialists advertise. If email is one channel among several, that is a sensible trade; our Kit vs Systeme.io comparison goes deeper on exactly this choice.

Mailchimp — the familiar name, on a smaller free plan

Mailchimp is the tool most people have heard of, and its strengths are real: a large template library, popup forms, and 300+ integrations included on every plan, so it connects to almost any booking, point-of-sale, or website tool a local business already uses. Two facts deserve attention before you choose it. First, the free plan has shrunk: it now covers up to 250 contacts with a maximum of 500 sends per month (or 250 per day), one seat and one audience, email support only for the first 30 days, and sending pauses if you exceed the limits. A café that collects a few addresses a week will pass 250 contacts within months. Second, paid pricing is by contact count, and your monthly sending allowance is a multiple of your contacts — 10× on Essentials, 12× on Standard, 15× on Premium. Essentials allows up to four steps per automation flow, while Standard and Premium allow up to 200 steps per flow; Standard and Essentials both offer a 14-day free trial. Mailchimp remains a reasonable choice if you need a specific integration only it offers — otherwise, compare what the same money buys elsewhere first.

Brevo — pay for sends, not for your list

Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) flips the usual pricing logic: you pay for emails sent, not contacts stored. That shows most clearly in the free plan, which — per Brevo's help center — lets you store up to 100,000 contacts and send 300 emails per day, with no time limit and no credit card required. For a business with a large, quiet customer database that sends one campaign a month, that model is dramatically cheaper than per-contact billing. The daily cap is the real constraint: a single campaign to 2,000 customers would take a week to trickle out on the free plan, since unused sends do not roll over. Paid plans start at $9/month (Starter, from 5,000 emails/month), and the Standard plan ($18/month) adds marketing automation, A/B testing, and landing pages. One limit sits behind that headline price, though: each paid volume tier also caps how many contacts you can store — 500 contacts on the 5,000-email tier, 1,500 on the 10,000-email tier, 2,500 on the 15,000-email tier, and 500,000 from the 20,000-email tier upward. For a business with a large list, the tier can end up set by its contact count rather than by how much it sends. Brevo also offers SMS and WhatsApp campaigns and sales (CRM) tools, which may matter if you want customer messaging and email in one place.

The free plans, head to head

All four free plans are genuinely usable, but they are generous in different directions. Kit gives you the biggest audience (10,000 subscribers) and unlimited sends, but holds back automation until you pay. Brevo gives you the biggest address book (100,000 stored contacts) but meters sending to 300 a day. Systeme.io gives you the most kinds of tools — funnels, a course, an automation rule — inside a 2,000-contact limit. Mailchimp's free plan is now best understood as a trial with no expiry date: fine for your first 250 contacts, but plan on paying or moving once the list grows. For a local business starting from zero, the practical order is: pick the free plan whose limit you will hit last, because moving a list later is possible but annoying.

Which one fits your business?

  • You want to grow a list and send a monthly newsletter, nothing more. Kit's free plan. Ten thousand subscribers and unlimited broadcasts cover years of growth; upgrade only when you want automated sequences.
  • You already have a big customer list but send rarely. Brevo. On the free plan, storing up to 100,000 contacts is free, and you pay for sending volume only when you actually send — just keep in mind that paid tiers cap stored contacts too, so a large list can push you above the $9 starting tier.
  • You need pages that take bookings or sell packages, plus email. Systeme.io. Funnels, a website, and email live in one account from the free plan up, and there is no platform fee on what you sell.
  • Your booking or shop system integrates with one tool only. Mailchimp, if that tool is Mailchimp — its 300+ integrations are its best argument. Go in knowing the free plan is small and paid pricing follows your contact count.
  • You send time-sensitive offers to your whole list at once. Avoid Brevo's free plan (the 300/day cap spreads one campaign across days) and Mailchimp's free plan (500 sends a month). Kit or Systeme.io, both with unlimited sends, fit this pattern even for free.

Bottom line

For local businesses, email software is a utility, not a strategy: the best tool is the one whose free plan lasts longest and whose billing model matches how you actually send. Kit leads for list-building, Brevo for large lists mailed occasionally, Systeme.io for businesses that also need selling pages, and Mailchimp for businesses tied into its integration ecosystem. All four let you start at $0, so the lowest-risk move is to pick the one that matches your pattern, import your list, and send your first campaign this week — the software matters less than the habit.
