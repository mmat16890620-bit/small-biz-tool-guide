Kit vs Systeme.io: Email Specialist or All-in-One — Which Fits a Small Business?

Last fact-checked: November 7, 2026 · Based on each vendor's official pricing and documentation pages (sources at the end)

Kit and Systeme.io are often mentioned in the same breath, but they are built on different ideas. Kit (formerly ConvertKit) is an email marketing platform for creators: its center of gravity is your subscriber list — broadcasts, sequences, automations, landing pages, and selling digital products to that list. Systeme.io is a true all-in-one suite: sales funnels, email marketing, websites and blogs, online courses, communities, and even running your own affiliate program, all under one subscription.

So the honest question is not "which is better?" but "which shape fits your business?" If email is the engine of your marketing, Kit is the specialist. If you want one login that replaces a stack of separate tools, Systeme.io is the generalist. Below, we compare the two on pricing, free plans, email, funnels, automation, and selling costs — using only what each vendor publishes.

The short version: Choose Kit if your business runs on an email list — a newsletter, a creator audience, digital products — and you want deep email tooling with a generous free plan (up to 10,000 subscribers). Choose Systeme.io if you need funnels, a website, courses, or an affiliate program in the same account, or if budget matters most: its paid plans start at $17/month and its free plan already includes working funnels.

Pricing: two different ways of counting

Kit

Kit has three plans — Free, Creator, and Pro — and prices the paid plans by subscriber count. According to Kit's help center, Creator starts at $39/month and Pro at $79/month, both for up to 1,000 subscribers, with a 14-day free trial that needs no credit card. Annual billing takes two months off (Creator works out to $390/year, Pro to $790/year). As your list grows into higher subscriber tiers, the price rises with it, so a large list costs meaningfully more than the starting figures — check the tier for your list size on Kit's pricing page before budgeting. Kit charges only for confirmed subscribers, and there are no limits on how many emails you can send on any plan.

Systeme.io

Systeme.io has four plans — Free, Startup ($17/month), Webinar ($47/month), and Unlimited ($97/month) — and scales mainly by contacts and how many funnels, courses, and automation rules you can run. Startup covers 5,000 contacts and 10 funnels; Webinar covers 10,000 contacts and 50 funnels and adds evergreen webinars; Unlimited removes the caps. Annual billing gives two months free, and every plan — including Free — includes unlimited email sends and 0% platform transaction fees.

The practical difference: Kit's bill is tied to the size of your audience, Systeme.io's to the size of your operation. A creator with a fast-growing list will feel Kit's price climb; a business running many funnels and courses will feel Systeme.io's resource caps first.

Email marketing

Email is Kit's home turf. All plans include broadcasts (one-off campaigns), unlimited landing pages and opt-in forms, tagging and segmentation, and a newsletter website to host your content. The free plan is unusually roomy for list-building — up to 10,000 subscribers — but Kit's help center is clear that visual automations and email sequences are paid-plan features: the upgrade to Creator is what unlocks welcome sequences and behavior-based funnels of emails. Creator also removes Kit branding and adds 24/7 support; Pro layers on engagement scoring, deliverability reporting, a newsletter referral system, and content A/B testing.

Systeme.io's email is built to serve the rest of the suite: campaigns and newsletters go out to your contacts with unlimited sends on every plan, and email connects natively to its funnels, courses, and checkout. What it doesn't publish is the kind of email-specific depth Kit advertises — engagement scoring, deliverability breakdowns, referral tooling. If email is one channel among several, Systeme.io's email is designed to be enough. If email is the business, Kit publishes the deeper bench.

Funnels and landing pages

This is the starkest difference. Systeme.io is funnel-first: even the free plan includes 3 working sales funnels, and its builder covers order bumps, 1-click upsells, A/B tests, and deadline funnels, alongside a website and blog builder. Kit, by contrast, does not offer a funnel builder at all. What it offers instead — unlimited landing pages and forms on every plan, with templates and the ability to deliver a signup incentive automatically — is aimed at one job: getting people onto your list. Neither approach is wrong; they answer different questions. "How do I sell this product step by step?" is a Systeme.io question. "How do I grow and keep an audience?" is a Kit question.

Automation

Both platforms automate, but they meter it differently. On Systeme.io, automation rules and workflows exist on every plan — the free plan includes 1 automation rule, Startup raises that to 10, Webinar to 100, and Unlimited removes the cap. On Kit, automation is the paid tier's headline: the visual automation builder and automated sequences are unlimited on Creator and Pro, while the free plan is built around manual broadcasts. A small business that wants a simple "when someone buys, send this" workflow on day one, for free, gets it from Systeme.io. A business ready to pay for sophisticated, visual email journeys will find Kit's builder is the one designed specifically for that.

Selling products, courses, and fees

Kit lets you sell digital products, paid newsletters, and recurring subscriptions from the same account as your list, and keeps the fee simple: 3.5% + 30¢ per transaction on every plan, with card processing included in that figure. Systeme.io covers more kinds of selling — digital and physical products, online courses with unlimited students on paid tiers, communities, bookings — and charges 0% platform transaction fees, though your payment gateway (Stripe, PayPal, and others) still takes its own processing cut. Systeme.io also lets you run your own affiliate program on every plan, which Kit does not offer. If you sell courses or want customers to promote you, that difference alone can decide it.

The free plans, head to head

Both free plans are genuinely usable, which is rare. Kit's free plan holds up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited landing pages, forms, and broadcasts — five times Systeme.io's free contact limit — making it the stronger free offer purely for growing a list. Systeme.io's free plan holds 2,000 contacts but includes things Kit charges for or doesn't sell at all: 3 sales funnels, 1 online course, a blog, 1 custom domain, and 1 affiliate program, with unlimited email sends and no credit card required. Grow an audience for free: Kit. Launch a small product for free: Systeme.io.

Which one fits your business?

  • You run a newsletter, podcast, or creator business. Kit. Its pricing, features, and growth tools (recommendations network, referral system on Pro) are all pointed at audience-first businesses, and the free plan gives you a long runway.
  • You sell a course, coaching program, or digital products and need pages that sell them. Systeme.io. Funnels, checkout, courses, and an affiliate program live in one account, and the 0% platform fee keeps more of each sale.
  • You're a local business starting from zero. It depends on your first move. If your first move is "collect emails and send a monthly newsletter," start on Kit's free plan. If it's "put up a page and take bookings or sell a package," start on Systeme.io's free plan. Both are free until you outgrow them, so the cost of choosing is low — the real cost is rebuilding later, which is why the shape of the tool matters more than the starting price.
  • Budget is the deciding factor. Systeme.io's paid plans start lower ($17/month vs. $39/month) and its top listed plan is $97/month flat, while Kit's price climbs with your subscriber count. Kit's counterweight: you may not need to pay at all until your list passes 10,000 subscribers, if broadcasts and landing pages are all you use.

Bottom line

Kit and Systeme.io overlap just enough to be confusing and differ exactly where it counts. Kit is the email specialist: a generous free list-building plan, deep email automation and analytics on paid tiers, and simple built-in selling for digital products — priced by the size of your audience. Systeme.io is the all-in-one: funnels, email, courses, websites, and affiliate management in a single account at lower, flatter prices — metered by contacts and how much you build. Pick based on which job is at the center of your marketing, and the rest of the comparison falls into place.
